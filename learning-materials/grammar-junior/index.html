Grammar

Forms and examples for S1–S3 writing and speaking.

Core
Tenses — Present

Simple Present

Use: habits, facts, timetables.

Form: base verb / verb + -s/-es (he/she/it).

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
I / you / we / they I walk to school. I do not (don't) walk to school. Do you walk to school? Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
he / she / it She walks to school. She does not (doesn't) walk to school. Does she walk to school? Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

Tip: Use do/does in negatives and questions — not don't walks.

Simple Present (Verb to Be)

Use: identity, feelings, place, time.

Form: am / is / are.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
I I am (I'm) ready. I am not (I'm not) ready. Am I ready? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
he / she / it She is (She's) in the library. She is not (isn't) in the library. Is she in the library? Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.
you / we / they They are (They're) late. They are not (aren't) late. Are they late? Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.

Tip: No do/does with be — say Is she…? not Does she is…?

Present Continuous

Use: happening now; temporary actions; near future plans with a time.

Form: am/is/are + verb-ing.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
I I am (I'm) revising. I am not (I'm not) revising. Am I revising? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
he / she / it He is (He's) revising. He is not (isn't) revising. Is he revising? Yes, he is. / No, he isn't.
you / we / they We are (We're) revising. We are not (aren't) revising. Are we revising? Yes, we are. / No, we aren't.

Tip: Spell -ing carefully (run → running; write → writing; study → studying).

Present Perfect

Use: past action with a link to now (just, already, yet, ever, never, since, for).

Form: have/has + past participle.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
I / you / we / they I have (I've) finished. I have not (haven't) finished. Have you finished? Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
he / she / it She has (She's) finished. She has not (hasn't) finished. Has she finished? Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.

Tip: since + starting point (since 2020); for + length (for two years). Do not mix with a finished past time (yesterday) — use simple past then.

Tenses — Past

Simple Past

Use: finished actions at a clear past time.

Form: regular -ed / irregular past forms.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
all subjects We visited the museum. We did not (didn't) visit the museum. Did you visit the museum? Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.

Tip: After did, use the base verb (Did she go? not Did she went?).

Useful Vocabulary → Irregular Verbs

Simple Past (Verb to Be)

Use: past identity, feelings, place.

Form: was / were.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
I / he / she / it I was tired. I was not (wasn't) tired. Was I tired? Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.
you / we / they They were tired. They were not (weren't) tired. Were they tired? Yes, they were. / No, they weren't.

Tip: was with I/he/she/it; were with you/we/they. (If I were you… → Conditionals · Second.)

Past Continuous

Use: action in progress at a past time; background for another past action.

Form: was/were + verb-ing.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
I / he / she / it She was reading. She was not (wasn't) reading. Was she reading? Yes, she was. / No, she wasn't.
you / we / they We were reading. We were not (weren't) reading. Were you reading? Yes, we were. / No, we weren't.

Tip: Often pairs with when + simple past: I was walking when it started to rain.

(Past perfect → Stretch.)

Tenses — Future (Will + Be Going to Only)

Will

Use: decisions at the moment of speaking; offers; predictions without strong evidence.

Form: will + base verb.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
all subjects I will (I'll) help you. I will not (won't) help you. Will you help me? Yes, I will. / No, I won't.

Be Going to

Use: plans already decided; predictions with present evidence.

Form: am/is/are going to + base verb.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
I I am (I'm) going to join the club. I am not (I'm not) going to join the club. Am I going to join? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
he / she / it She is (She's) going to join. She is not (isn't) going to join. Is she going to join? Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.
you / we / they They are (They're) going to join. They are not (aren't) going to join. Are they going to join? Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.

Tip: Look outside — dark clouds → It's going to rain (evidence). Offer at the door → I'll open it (will).

Passive (Simple Present / Simple Past)

Use: when the action (or object) matters more than who did it.

Form: be + past participle. (by + agent) only when useful.

Simple Present Passive

Number Positive Negative Questions Short answers
singular The hall is cleaned every day. The hall is not (isn't) cleaned every day. Is the hall cleaned every day? Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.
plural The windows are cleaned every day. The windows are not (aren't) cleaned every day. Are the windows cleaned every day? Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.

Simple Past Passive

Number Positive Negative Questions Short answers
singular The hall was cleaned yesterday. The hall was not (wasn't) cleaned yesterday. Was the hall cleaned yesterday? Yes, it was. / No, it wasn't.
plural The windows were cleaned yesterday. The windows were not (weren't) cleaned yesterday. Were the windows cleaned yesterday? Yes, they were. / No, they weren't.

Tip: Keep the past participle (cleaned, written, done) — do not add -ed twice. Full 8-row passive table → Stretch.

Reported Speech (Statements + Yes/No)

Use: tell someone what another person said.

Form: reporting verb (said / told) + tense shift when the reporting verb is past.

Statements

Direct Reported
"I am tired," she said. She said (that) she was tired.
"We live in Kowloon," they said. They said (that) they lived in Kowloon.
"I finished my homework," he said. He said (that) he had finished his homework.
"I will help you," she said. She said (that) she would help me.

Tip: say + (that)… ; tell + person + (that)… (She told me that… not She told that…).

Yes/No Questions

Direct Reported
"Are you ready?" he asked. He asked if/whether I was ready.
"Do you like basketball?" she asked. She asked if/whether I liked basketball.
"Have you eaten?" they asked. They asked if/whether I had eaten.

Tip: Use if/whether — no question mark; statement word order (he asked if I was… not if was I).

(Reported Wh-questions → Stretch.)

Conditionals 0 · 1 · 2

Zero Conditional

Use: facts and routines that are always true.

Form: If + present, present.

  • If you heat ice, it melts.
  • If the bell rings, we go to class.

First Conditional

Use: real future possibility.

Form: If + present, will + base.

  • If we start early, we will finish on time.
  • If it rains, the match will be cancelled.

Tip: Present after if — not will in both halves.

Second Conditional

Use: unreal / imaginary present or future.

Form: If + past, would + base.

  • If I had more time, I would join the choir.
  • If every class joined, we would raise more funds.

Tip: If I were you… is the fixed polite form (British classroom standard).

(Type 3 + Mixed → Stretch.)

Transitional Words

Short cohesion words help writing flow.

  • however
  • therefore
  • for example
  • in addition

Present Perfect Continuous

Use: action started in the past and still continuing / just stopped, with focus on duration.

Form: have/has been + verb-ing.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
I / you / we / they I have been waiting. I have not (haven't) been waiting. Have you been waiting? Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
he / she / it She has been waiting. She has not (hasn't) been waiting. Has she been waiting? Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.

Tip: Often with for / since / all morning. Prefer present perfect simple for finished results (I've finished); continuous for the ongoing activity.

Past Perfect Continuous

Use: longer action continuing up to a past point.

Form: had been + verb-ing.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
all subjects They had been practising for an hour. They had not (hadn't) been practising. Had they been practising? Yes, they had. / No, they hadn't.
Future Continuous

Use: action in progress at a future time.

Form: will be + verb-ing.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
all subjects This time tomorrow I will be sitting the exam. I will not (won't) be sitting the exam. Will you be sitting the exam? Yes, I will. / No, I won't.
Future Perfect

Use: action completed before a future time.

Form: will have + past participle.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
all subjects By June we will have finished the project. We will not (won't) have finished. Will you have finished by June? Yes, we will. / No, we won't.
Past Perfect

Use: the earlier of two past actions (already, before, after, by the time).

Form: had + past participle.

Person Positive Negative Questions Short answers
all subjects She had left before I arrived. She had not (hadn't) left. Had she left? Yes, she had. / No, she hadn't.

Tip: Simple past for the later action; past perfect for the earlier one.

Reported Wh-Questions
Direct Reported
"Where do you live?" she asked. She asked where I lived.
"What are you doing?" he asked. He asked what I was doing.
"When did you arrive?" they asked. They asked when I had arrived.
"Why have you come?" she asked. She asked why I had come.

Tip: Keep the Wh-word; use statement order (where I lived not where did I live).

Conditionals Type 3 + Mixed

Type 3

Use: unreal past (regret / missed chance).

Form: If + past perfect, would have + past participle.

  • If we had left earlier, we would have caught the bus.
  • If she had revised, she would have passed.

Mixed

Use: past condition with a present result (or reverse where teaching needs it).

Form (common): If + past perfect, would + base.

  • If I had slept more last night, I would feel better now.
  • If she had taken the job, she would be in London now.
Comparative / Superlative Adverbs

Regular

Base Comparative Superlative
fast faster (the) fastest
hard harder (the) hardest
carefully more carefully (the) most carefully
quietly more quietly (the) most quietly
early earlier (the) earliest

Tip: Short adverbs often take -er/-est; -ly adverbs usually take more/most.

Irregular

Base Comparative Superlative
well better (the) best
badly worse (the) worst
much more (the) most
little less (the) least
far farther / further (the) farthest / furthest

Tip: Adjective good → better/best; adverb well → better/best. Do not write more better.
