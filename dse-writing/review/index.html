Film / book / product

Review

Evaluate with reasons — not only a summary. Balanced praise and criticism.

P2 / P3B: Both. P2: invent suitable details about the item. P3B: use Data File facts and the exact item / criteria given. Keep it a review — judge with reasons, not only a summary.

PAT
PurposeEvaluate a film, book, product, place, or event; help readers decide.
AudienceMagazine / website / school readers who want a clear judgement.
ToneSemi-formal; lively OK. Balanced: praise and criticism with reasons.
Structure

Opening

  • Title of the review / name the item
  • Brief context (what it is; who it is for)
  • End with thesis (overall judgement) — last sentence of the opening paragraph

Body

  • 2–4 paragraphs (plot/features → strengths → weaknesses, or by criteria)
  • First sentence of each = topic sentence (one main idea only)
  • Then reason + example (after the topic sentence)
  • Avoid long spoiler dumps

Closing

  • Restate overall judgement
  • Who you recommend it to (or not)
  • Optional star rating if the task allows
Skeleton

Slots: [thesis / angle] · [topic / point] · [detail / example] · [other]

Opening

Review of [title / product]

[Item] is a [type] aimed at [audience]. Overall, I would say it is [thesis — judgement].

Body

First, [strength / feature 1]. This works well because [reason]. For example, [detail].

Second, [strength / feature 2]. [Evidence].

However, [weakness]. This may disappoint readers who [expectation].

Closing

All in all, [restate judgement]. I would recommend it to [who], but not to [who].

[Optional: ★★★☆☆]
Useful phrases
  • This [film / book / product] is worth a closer look because…
  • One of its greatest strengths is…
  • What stood out most was…
  • On the downside,…
  • Critics may argue that…; yet…
  • Compared with [similar item],…
  • I would rate it… / I would award it…
  • I highly recommend it to anyone who…
