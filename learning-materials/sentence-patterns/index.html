Sentence patterns

Pick 2–3 patterns per task. Also useful for DSE Writing Formats.

Tap a rung to open it. Basic starts open for weaker classes; Building and Exam-ready stay collapsed on a phone. Tap 🛠 (bottom-right) for ✍️ Handwrite notes. 譯 Chinese glosses will be added later. Format tags are teaching hints — not a checklist.

Basic

Short, clear frames. One idea per sentence. Safe for every format.

Subject + Verb + Object

Many students take the MTR to school.

Tip: Keep subject short; add one object only.

  • Article
  • Blog
  • Report

There is / There are

There are three computer rooms in our school.

Tip: Use are with plural nouns (rooms, clubs).

  • Report
  • Proposal
  • Article

Simple present

Our school holds a charity fair every year.

Tip: Use for habits and facts — not yesterday’s story.

  • Article
  • Blog
  • Formal letter / email

Simple past

Last week we visited the waterfront.

Tip: One past time word (yesterday, last week) is enough.

  • Blog
  • Speech
  • Article

can / should / must

Students should revise before the exam.

Tip: must = strong rule; should = advice; can = ability or possibility.

  • Speech
  • Formal letter / email
  • Proposal
Building

Join ideas and add a little detail. Still keep each sentence easy to follow.

because / when

We stayed after school because we had a rehearsal.

Tip: Put the reason after because; keep both halves short.

  • Blog
  • Speech
  • Formal letter / email

if / although

Although the weather was wet, the fair went well.

Tip: Comma after the although / if half when it comes first.

  • Article
  • Speech
  • Letter to the editor

who / which / that

The teacher who organised the trip was helpful.

Tip: who = people; which / that = things. One clause is enough.

  • Article
  • Review
  • Report

to + infinitive (purpose)

We practised hard to prepare for the contest.

Tip: to + verb answers “why?” — no for to.

  • Proposal
  • Speech
  • Report

Passive (is / was + V-ed)

The hall was cleaned after the assembly.

Tip: Use when the action matters more than who did it.

  • Report
  • Proposal
  • Formal letter / email

First conditional (If + present, will…)

If we start early, we will finish on time.

Tip: Present after if; will in the other half — not will in both.

  • Proposal
  • Speech
  • Formal letter / email
Exam-ready

Stretch patterns for stronger marks. Use one or two per essay — not every sentence.

Participle phrase

Walking past the harbour, we saw the evening lights.

Tip: The -ing half and the main half share the same subject.

  • Article
  • Blog
  • Speech

Second conditional (If + past, would…)

If every class joined, we would raise more funds.

Tip: For unreal / imaginary ideas — not real plans you will do tomorrow.

  • Proposal
  • Speech
  • Letter to the editor

Hedging — It seems that…

It seems that more students prefer morning revision.

Tip: Softens a claim when you lack hard proof.

  • Report
  • Article
  • Letter to the editor

Hedging — It is widely believed that…

It is widely believed that sleep helps exam performance.

Tip: Good for general views; still add one concrete local detail.

  • Article
  • Speech
  • Letter to the editor

Hedging — It is recommended / suggested that…

It is recommended that the club meet twice a week.

Tip: Soft advice for decision-makers; then give one clear reason.

  • Proposal
  • Report
  • Formal letter / email

Emphasis — What we need is…

What we need is a clearer timetable for after-school clubs.

Tip: Put the key noun after is — one clear need only.

  • Proposal
  • Speech
  • Letter to the editor

Emphasis — Not only… but also…

Not only did the team win, but they also raised money for charity.

Tip: After Not only invert the verb (did the team…); keep both halves parallel.
