Paper Map

Weighting & time

  • Paper 4 Speaking = 10% of the subject (SBA is separate)
  • Exam: Group Interaction then Individual Response

Group Interaction (GI)

  • Usually 3–4 candidates; about 8 minutes for a group of 4 (shorter for 3)
  • Discuss a given topic / situation; reach ideas or a decision as the task asks

Individual Response (IR)

  • About 1 minute after GI
  • Examiner asks one question related to the discussion; you answer alone

How you are marked

  • Pronunciation & delivery — clear enough to follow; natural pace
  • Communication strategies — turn-taking, clarifying, inviting others, repairing
  • Vocabulary & language patterns — suitable range; not only set phrases
  • Ideas & organisation — relevant points; extend with reasons / examples; IR has a clear line
Group Interaction

Opening & defining the task

  • Greet briefly; restate what you must decide / discuss
  • Keep the opening short so the group can start ideas
  • Check everyone shares the same task focus
Example

Open Day booth plan → “Shall we decide three activities and who runs them?” — clear task, not a long speech.

Turn-taking

  • Invite quiet members; don’t monopolise
  • Link to others’ points (“As Mei said…”) before adding yours
  • Leave space; short turns beat one long monologue
Example

One student talks for two minutes on MTR crowding → invite: “Ken, what do you think about peak-hour trains?”

Extending ideas

  • Reason + example; don’t stop at one short sentence
  • Build on the topic — why it matters, what could happen
  • Keep examples local and clear
Example

“Cha chaan teng jobs help students” → add: flexible hours after school + real customer practice — not just “it’s good”.

Agreeing & disagreeing politely

  • Soft disagree + alternative (“I see your point, but… / Another option is…”)
  • Agree with a reason, not only “yes”
  • Stay on the task — don’t turn disagreement personal
Example

Partner wants a Lion Rock hike for the class picnic → “That sounds fun, but after a typhoon the trails may be unsafe — maybe a harbourfront walk instead?”

Clarifying & repairing

  • Ask for repeat / meaning when you miss something
  • Paraphrase to check understanding
  • Repair your own slip quickly and move on
Example

Miss a name in the prompt → “Sorry — do you mean the SCMP letter or the school notice?”

Summarising & closing

  • Short wrap if the group is ready
  • Restate the decision / main ideas in one or two lines
  • Don’t force a long summary if time is already gone
Example

“So we’ve chosen three Open Day stalls — games, food, and a photo corner — and each of us will lead one.”

Individual Response

IR structure

  • Clear stance → 2 reasons / examples → short close
  • Stay on the examiner’s question — not a second GI
  • 1 minute is short; one clear answer beats a scattered list
Example

“Should schools start later?” → stance + two reasons (travel time on MTR; sleep) + one-line close.

Keeping English going

  • Paraphrase when stuck; avoid long Cantonese
  • Fillers sparingly (well / I mean) — don’t fill every gap
  • Simple clear English > silence or a memorised speech that misses the topic
Example

Forget “volunteer” → “students who help at Open Day” — keep talking in English.

Linking back to the GI

  • You may use an idea from the discussion, but answer the examiner’s question directly
  • Don’t restart the whole group topic
Example

GI was about Open Day stalls → IR asks which skill students learn → name one skill + reason; don’t re-list all stalls.

Exam Habits

Prep notes

  • Eyes up when speaking; notes only as a quick prompt

Body language

  • Look at group members in GI; look at the examiner for IR

Balance

  • Speak enough to be assessed; leave space for others in GI

IR

  • Listen to the question; 1 minute is short — one clear answer, not a second GI

Don’t memorise a whole speech

  • Adapt set phrases to the topic (see Speaking Expressions)

If you blank

  • Repeat / paraphrase the question and give one simple point first
