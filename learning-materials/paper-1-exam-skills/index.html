Paper Map

Weighting & time

  • Paper 1 Reading = 20% of the subject
  • 1½ hours for Part A + Part B together

Part A (Compulsory) — 10%

  • Everyone sits Part A
  • One or more texts; topics and genres vary
  • Skills mix: easier and harder items together
  • Formats: MC, short answers, open-ended

Part B — choose B1 or B2 (each 10%)

B1 (easier) B2 (more difficult)
Texts More straightforward More challenging
Demand Less cognitively demanding More cognitively demanding
Highest level Level 4 (A+B1) Full range, including Level 5 / 5**
  • Part B tests the same skills as Part A, at different difficulty

Timing

  • One clock for A + your B — leave a clear block for B
  • Decide B1 or B2 before exam day (with your teacher) — not under panic in the room

Item types

gap-fill / summary cloze · T/F/NG · matching · pronoun referencing · MC (4) · chart/table · ordering · open-ended / short response

Reading Skills

Skimming

  • Read title, subtitles, first/last paragraphs, topic sentences → quick “map”
  • Ask: main idea? audience? writer’s purpose?
  • Don’t stop for every unknown word on the first pass
Example

Weekend hiking at Lion Rock — skim once → safety & trail etiquette, not a history of the peak.

Scanning

  • Know the keyword (name, date, number, place) before you search
  • Hunt that word / a clear synonym; ignore the rest until you hit it
  • Check the sentence really answers the question
Example

MTR exit for a Saturday market → scan exit / station / Saturday, then read that line only.

Main Idea

  • Prefer the topic sentence over colourful examples
  • Answer covers the whole paragraph/text — not one detail
  • MC trap: options that reuse words from a side example
Example

Three reasons for a cha chaan teng job → main idea = why the job attracts students, not milk-tea prices.

Detail

  • Match keywords to the right paragraph; answers are often short
  • Watch synonyms (teenagers ↔ Form 4 students)
  • Copy carefully when the instruction allows words from the text
Example

“ONE benefit … in paragraph 3” → lift from paragraph 3, not paragraph 5.

Inferring

  • Answer is suggested, not spelled out (imply, suggest, most likely)
  • Stay inside the text — no general-knowledge guesses
  • Use your own words when asked to explain
Example

Typhoon signal “saved” picnic plans → infer relief / gratitude, not love of typhoons.

Vocabulary in Context

  • Read the sentence before and after the target word
  • Check part of speech / tense / number so the synonym fits
  • MC: swap each option in — which fits the tone?
Example

“The queue … was brutal” → very tough / severe, not “violent crime”.

Tone / Attitude

  • Tone = how the writer sounds; attitude = stance toward the topic
  • Prefer precise labels (sceptical, approving, ironic) over vague positive/negative
  • Evidence: word choice, examples, what is praised or criticised
Example

Path called “long overdue” and “a gift to walkers” → approving / supportive.

Views / Opinions

  • Separate facts from someone’s view (writer, interviewee, critic)
  • Yes/No + evidence: pick a stance you can support, then cite it
  • Two sides asked → both reasons must be in the passage
Example

Shop owner welcomes tourists; resident worries about noise → two text-based views.

Cohesion / Reference

  • Pronouns (this, they, it) and linkers point back (sometimes forward)
  • Check the sentence before; match gender / number
  • Answer with a clear noun phrase — not a whole sentence unless asked
Example

“Many residents opposed the plan. This delayed the vote.” → This = their opposition to the plan.

Question Types
  • Factual / detail — stated info; short answers from the right lines
  • Referencing — what pronouns and referring words point to
  • True / False / Not Given — matches, contradicts, or goes beyond the text
  • Multiple choice — cut distractors; match stem to the passage
  • Matching / ordering — headings, speakers, or events in sequence
  • Chart / table — accurate, grammar-fit details in organisers
  • Gap-fill / summary cloze — meaning + word form; don’t repeat the summary’s idea
  • Open-ended / short response — clear and complete; don’t over-copy

Exam Habits

B1 or B2

  • Match your ability — ask your teacher before the exam
  • Level 5 usually needs B2; B1 caps at Level 4 (A+B1)

Instructions

  • Read every instruction (word limits, “ONE word”, “from paragraph 4”, “use a phrase from the text”)

Words from the text

  • Use them when told — don’t dump whole sentences into every blank

Open-ended

  • Answer the question, then stop — extra irrelevant detail hurts clarity

Gap-fill / cloze

  • Check meaning + word form (tense, number, part of speech)
  • Re-read the finished paragraph

Summary

  • Use both the original and the summary — don’t repeat what the summary already says

Accuracy

  • Spelling / punctuation matter when a change alters meaning
  • Give all required elements (e.g. TWO examples = two)

Timing

  • 1½ hours for A + B — leave time to check blanks and careless slips
