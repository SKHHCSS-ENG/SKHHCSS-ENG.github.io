Paper 1 - Reading

Paper 1 - Exam Skills
  • Paper 1 is 20% of the subject; 1½ hours for Part A and Part B together.
  • Part A is compulsory (10%). Part B: choose B1 (easier; A+B1 caps at Level 4) or B2 (full range, including Level 5 / 5**).
  • Decide B1 or B2 with your teacher before exam day, and leave a clear block of time for Part B.
  • Skim first for a map (title, subtitles, first and last paragraphs); scan only after you know the keyword.
  • Main idea covers the whole paragraph, not one colourful detail. For inference, stay inside the text.
  • Watch synonyms, word limits, and “from paragraph X”. In gap-fill, check meaning and word form.
  • Open-ended: answer the question, then stop. Leave time to check spelling and every required element.

Paper 2 - Writing

Paper 2 - Exam Skills
  • Paper 2 is 25% of the subject; 2 hours for Parts A and B together.
  • Part A is compulsory (~200 words, 10%). Part B: choose 1 of 4 questions (~400 words, 15%).
  • Choose Part B by a topic and genre you can finish well, and mark your choice with an X in the answer book.
  • You are marked on Content, Language, and Organisation.
  • Before you write, be clear on purpose, audience, and tone, and tick off every task bullet.
  • Plan for a few minutes (3–4 points); one idea per paragraph; clear language beats rare words used wrongly.
  • Classroom timing guide (not HKEAA): about 40 minutes for A, 70–75 minutes for B, then a short check.
  • Word guides are targets, not automatic deductions. Leave 3–5 minutes to check bullets, genre features, tone, and slips.

Paper 2 - Writing Formats
  • Always follow the task’s stated format. Other Part A genres (email, blog, questionnaire) may also appear.
  • Letter to the Editor — public argument or complaint; thesis last in the opening; Yours faithfully / sincerely.
  • Formal letter or email — one reader or organisation (request, complain, apply).
  • Article — hook and angle; no letter sign-off. Blog — conversational; no Dear… / Yours…
  • Review — overall judgement, with strengths and weaknesses.
  • Proposal — aims, details, resources, benefits; ask for approval.
  • Report — facts under headings; findings, then recommendations.
  • Speech — write for the ear: greet, thesis, signposted points, thank the audience.

Paper 3 - Listening & Integrated Skills

Paper 3 - Exam Skills

  • Paper 3 is 30% of the subject; about 2 hours.
  • Part A is compulsory listening. Part B: choose B1 or B2 — listening plus a Data File, then one extended writing task.
  • B1 is more straightforward (A+B1 caps at Level 4). B2 is more demanding (full range, including Level 5 / 5**). Decide with your teacher before exam day.
  • Part B writing is marked on Content, Language, Organisation, and Appropriacy (register, tone, and format).
  • Before the audio, read the questions and underline keywords. Listen for gist and for detail (names, numbers, times).
  • Take short, readable notes. In Part B, skim the Data File first, then listen with purpose, then write.
  • Use only the Data File and the recording — don’t invent facts. Copy names and places carefully.
  • Cover every required bullet, and leave time to check facts, tone, and copy errors.

Paper 4 - Speaking

Paper 4 - Exam Skills
  • Paper 4 is 10% of the subject (SBA is separate). Group Interaction first, then Individual Response.
  • GI: usually 3–4 candidates, about 8 minutes for a group of 4. IR: about 1 minute, one question from the examiner.
  • You are marked on pronunciation and delivery, communication strategies, vocabulary and language patterns, and ideas and organisation.
  • Open GI by restating the task, then take short turns: invite others, link to their points, and add a reason or example.
  • Disagree politely and offer an alternative. Clarify if you miss something; repair a slip and move on.
  • IR: clear stance, two reasons or examples, short close. Answer the examiner’s question — not a second GI.
  • Keep English going: paraphrase if you are stuck. Don’t memorise a whole speech.
  • Look at the group in GI and at the examiner in IR. If you blank, repeat the question and give one simple point first.

Junior Grammar

Core forms for S1–S3:

  • Simple present — habits, facts, timetables; base / -s. Use do/does in negatives and questions (not don’t walks).
  • Verb to be (am/is/are) — no do/does with be (Is she…? not Does she is…?).
  • Present continuous — happening now or a near-future plan; am/is/are + -ing.
  • Present perfect — past with a link to now (just, already, yet, since, for); have/has + past participle. Don’t mix with a finished past time (yesterday) — use simple past then.
  • Simple past — finished past time; after did, use the base verb (Did she go?).
  • was/were — was with I/he/she/it; were with you/we/they.
  • Past continuous — was/were + -ing; often I was walking when it started to rain.
  • Will — decisions now, offers, predictions without strong evidence.
  • Be going to — plans already decided, or predictions with present evidence (dark clouds → It’s going to rain).
  • Passive (simple present / simple past) — when the action matters more than who did it: be + past participle (The hall is cleaned / was cleaned). Don’t add -ed twice.
  • Reported speech — say + (that)…; tell + person + (that)…. Shift the tense when the reporting verb is past. Yes/no questions: if/whether + statement word order (he asked if I was ready).
  • Conditionals 0 · 1 · 2 — Zero: If + present, present (facts). First: If + present, will + base (don’t put will in both halves). Second: If + past, would + base; If I were you… is the usual polite form.
  • Transitional words — however, therefore, for example, in addition.

Stretch topics (full tables and extra forms) are on the Junior Grammar page: perfect continuous, future continuous and perfect, past perfect, the full passive table, reported Wh-questions, conditionals Type 3 and mixed, and comparative / superlative adverbs.
