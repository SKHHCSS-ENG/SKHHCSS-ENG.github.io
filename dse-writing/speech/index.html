P2 / P3B

Speech

Write for the ear. Warmer and more direct than a report.

P2 / P3B: Both. P2: school / community speech. P3B: use audience, occasion, and key points from the Data File.

PAT
PurposeInform, persuade, motivate, or thank — as the occasion requires.
AudienceListeners in front of you (students, teachers, guests). Write for the ear.
ToneFormal enough for the occasion, but warmer and more direct than a report.
Structure

Opening

  • Greet the right people
  • Introduce yourself if needed
  • End with thesis (central message) — last sentence of the opening

Body

  • 2–4 clear points
  • First sentence of each = spoken topic sentence + signpost (First…, Next…) — one main idea only
  • Then reason + example (after the topic sentence)

Closing

  • Restate central message
  • Memorable final line
  • Thank the audience
Skeleton

Slots: [thesis / angle] · [topic / point] · [detail / example] · [other]

Opening

Good [morning / afternoon], [audience].
My name is [Name], and today I would like to talk about [topic].
My main message is that [thesis].

Body

First, [main idea 1]. This matters because [reason]. For example, [example].
Second, [main idea 2]. If we [action], we can [result].
Finally, [main idea 3 / call to action]. Together, we can [hope / goal].

Closing

To sum up, [restate central idea].
Thank you for your kind attention.
Useful phrases
  • Good morning / afternoon, [audience].
  • It is my honour to speak to you today about…
  • Have you ever thought about…?
  • The first point I would like to make is…
  • Equally important,…
  • Let me give you a simple example.
  • What can we do, then?
  • I hope you will join me in…
  • Thank you for your kind attention.
