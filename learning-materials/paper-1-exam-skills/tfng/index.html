True / False / Not Given

Match, contradict, or go beyond the text — stay inside the passage.

True / False / Not Given

What It Asks

  • True (T) — the claim is stated in the text (same meaning; wording may differ)
  • False (F) — the claim contradicts the text
  • Not Given (NG) — not enough information in the text
  • Do not use outside knowledge — only the passage

How To Decide

  • Find the relevant lines before you mark
  • Check each claim word-by-word (numbers, names, absolute words)
  • Absolute words (always / never / all / only) often trap — does the text really say that?
  • Mark NG if the text is silent, or only related but does not settle the claim
  • Synonyms OK for T — same meaning counts

Common Traps

  • Almost true — one wrong detail (date, number, person, place)
  • True in real life / general knowledge, but not in the passage → NG or F only if the text contradicts
  • Reversed cause, time, or who did what
  • Stronger / weaker than the text (must vs may; all vs some)

Quick Check

  • Did I find the lines that matter — or guess from memory?
  • Does every key word in the claim fit the text?
  • Is anything in the claim missing from the text? → lean NG
  • Am I adding my own knowledge? → stop; use the passage only
  • Absolute word present? Check it hard before T

Mini Example

Example

Passage
Our school library opens at 7:45 a.m. on weekdays. Form 4 students may borrow up to four books for two weeks. Magazines stay in the reading corner and cannot be taken home. The library closes early on the last Friday of each month for staff training.

Statement 1 — Form 4 students may borrow four books for two weeks.
T — stated (same meaning)

Statement 2 — Magazines can be taken home for one week.
F — text says magazines cannot be taken home

Statement 3 — The library opens earlier on exam days.
NG — exam days not mentioned
