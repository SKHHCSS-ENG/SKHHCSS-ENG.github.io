Paper 2 - Writing

HKDSE English Language · study notes overview

Paper Map

Weighting & time

  • Paper 2 Writing = 25% of the subject
  • 2 hours for Parts A and B together

Part A (Compulsory) — 10%

  • Everyone sits Part A — one short, guided task
  • About 200 words; situation, purpose, and some relevant information are given
  • Follow the prompts / bullet points in the question — cover what is asked
  • Genres vary (e.g. short article, email, blog, questionnaire responses) — read the task carefully

Part B — choose 1 of 4 questions — 15%

  • Longer, more open-ended task — about 400 words
  • From 2024 onwards: 4 questions (was 8) — pick the one you can write best
  • Choose by topic + genre you know, not by “looks easy”
  • Mark the chosen question with an X on the question paper (HKEAA sample: the question-number box in the Question-Answer Book)

How you are marked

Same criteria for A and B; marked independently, then combined.

Criterion What markers look for
Content Task fulfilled; relevant, developed ideas; audience awareness; creativity when appropriate
Language Accurate grammar; varied sentence patterns; well-chosen vocabulary; register / tone / style fit the genre
Organisation Clear overall structure; logical paragraphs; cohesion suited to the text type

Timing

  • One clock for A + B — leave a clear block for the longer Part B
  • Suggested split (classroom guide, not HKEAA rule): ~40 min A · ~70–75 min B · leftover for plan / check
  • Use the rough-work sheet for a short plan — do not write the whole script there
Writing Skills

Use these on every Paper 2 task. Format pages show how each genre applies them.

Purpose · Audience · Tone

  • Purpose — what must this text do? (inform, persuade, propose, complain, entertain…)
  • Audience — who reads it? (editor / public, principal, classmates, customers)
  • Tone / register — formal, semi-formal, or friendly? Stay consistent end to end
  • Match genre features (greeting, headline, headings, sign-off) to that purpose and audience
Example

Letter to the SCMP about MTR crowding → purpose = persuade + complain; audience = editor / public; tone = formal, calm, evidence-based — not chatty WhatsApp.

Task Fulfilment

  • Underline every requirement in the question (role, reader, points to cover, word guide)
  • Tick them off as you draft — missing a bullet costs Content marks
  • Stay on the asked topic; do not invent a different task
  • Part A: use the given information; Part B: develop your own ideas with enough support
Example

Part A asks for three improvements to a school Open Day → give three clear, developed points, not one long story.

Planning

  • 3–5 minutes: jot purpose / audience / 3–4 main points before writing
  • Decide paragraph order (problem → reasons → suggestion; or intro → body → close)
  • For Part B, choose the question you can finish well — not the flashiest title

Paragraphing

  • One clear idea per paragraph; open with a topic sentence when it helps the reader
  • Short intro that sets context; body that develops; ending that fits the genre (call to action, summary, polite close)
  • Avoid one giant block or a string of one-sentence “paragraphs”

Cohesion

  • Link ideas with precise connectives (however, therefore, in addition) — not a stack of and / and / and
  • Use pronouns and synonyms to avoid clumsy repetition
  • Keep tense and viewpoint steady unless the genre needs a shift

Language for Marks

  • Prefer clear over showy — HKEAA warns against over-ambitious language that confuses the reader
  • Mix simple and complex sentences; check subject–verb agreement and article use
  • Vocabulary: exact words for the topic (school / city / workplace) beat rare words used wrongly
  • Spelling and punctuation matter when a slip changes meaning
Formats

Five core formats are cards on this page (plus Formal letter, Blog and Review). Full PAT, structure and skeleton notes live on each format page.

  • Letter to the Editor — public argument or complaint to a newspaper / magazine editor
  • Article — magazine or school-publication piece to inform, discuss, or entertain a wide readership
  • Proposal — suggest a plan or project to a decision-maker (principal, committee, NGO)
  • Report — present findings / situation / recommendations in a clear, factual layout
  • Speech — spoken-style address to a live audience (assembly, ceremony, competition)

Other Part A genres may appear (email, blog, questionnaire). Always follow the task’s stated format.

Exam Habits

HKEAA briefing / seminar tips for Paper 2.

Part B choice

  • Choose Part B wisely — topic and genre you practise; not panic-picking in the room

Before you write

  • Spend a few minutes on ideas + text type + audience before you write

Clarity

  • Clarity first — do not force rare words or ultra-long sentences that hide your meaning

Stay on task

  • Stay on task purpose; every paragraph should earn Content marks

Timing

  • Manage the 2 hours: finish both parts with time to re-read for missing bullets and slips

Answer book

  • Write on every line of the answer book to avoid extra sheets where possible
  • Use the rough-work sheet for planning; transfer a clean answer into the book
  • In Part B, put an X next to the question you chose

Word guides

  • Word guides (~200 / ~400) are targets for a full sample of your writing — HKEAA marks positively (no automatic deduction for going over), but empty padding does not help

Final check

  • Leave 3–5 minutes to check: all required points? Genre features? Tone consistent? Obvious grammar slips?
