Paper 1 - Past Paper Vocabulary

Practise working out word meaning from context — a key Paper 1 skill. Each year is an original practice set, not a list from that year’s exam. Tap a word for its part of speech, an English definition, and an example. Use 譯 for the Chinese meaning.

Use any clue that helps: contrast, example, result, restatement, or inference.

  • resilient
  • compromise
  • feasible
  • escalate
  • advocate

Writers often judge an idea as strong or weak. Use the reason in the same sentence to confirm the word.

  • flawed
  • compelling
  • dubious
  • constructive
  • superficial

These words limit or widen a statement. Read the example that follows before you settle on a meaning.

  • predominantly
  • merely
  • sparse
  • ample
  • solely

Sometimes no phrase restates the word. Use the details in the sentence to infer the meaning.

  • grim
  • strained
  • lukewarm
  • hasty
  • grudging

Some verbs have typical partners. Learn the whole phrase, then use the rest of the sentence to confirm the meaning.

  • pose a threat
  • play a role
  • raise awareness
  • take into account
  • reach a consensus

In Paper 1, a more formal word often appears in reports, notices and official letters. Use 譯 for the everyday Chinese meaning.

  • commence
  • obtain
  • reside
  • cease
  • request

Words of degree tell you how much. Compare them with the figure or example in the same sentence.

  • marginally
  • substantially
  • negligible
  • drastic
  • relatively

This, these, such and a following clause often point back to the word and show what it stands for.

  • initiative
  • phenomenon
  • setback
  • claim
  • measure

The words around a description often show how the writer feels. Use that tone to pin down the word.

  • sarcastic
  • sympathetic
  • dismissive
  • optimistic
  • sceptical

Break a long word into parts. A prefix or suffix often carries a reliable meaning (un- = not, over- = too much, bi- = two).

  • unpredictable
  • overestimate
  • bilingual
  • irreversible
  • underestimate

Writers often restate a word with in other words, that is, namely, a dash, or a second clause that means the same thing.

  • predicament
  • compact
  • conventional
  • vital
  • compile

Look for because, so, as a result and similar links. They often show what the word does, or what it leads to.

  • trigger
  • deteriorate
  • incentive
  • undermine
  • boost

Words such as such as, for instance, including and along with often introduce examples that explain the word.

  • amenities
  • staple
  • appliances
  • vocation
  • commodities

When a sentence turns with but, however, although or unlike, the word often means the opposite of what comes on the other side.

  • reluctant
  • scarce
  • adverse
  • obsolete
  • drawback
