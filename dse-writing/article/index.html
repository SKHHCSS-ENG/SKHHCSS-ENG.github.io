Newspaper / magazine / school magazine

Article

Inform, explain, argue, or hold readers’ interest. Not a letter — no Dear… / Yours…

Tap to show Chinese glosses for harder words (marked in the text).

P2 / P3B: Both. P2: topic + target outlet. P3B: Data File facts + exact outlet (school / local / youth magazine).

PAT

PurposeInform, explain, argue, or hold readers’ interest about a topic.
AudienceMatch the outlet (public, students, parents).
ToneSemi-formal to formal; school magazine can be slightly warmer — still clear, not chatty.

Structure ‖ Skeleton

Structure

Opening

  • Catchy title (if asked)
  • Hook + name the topic
  • End with thesis / angle (last sentence of the opening paragraph)

Body

  • 3–4 paragraphs
  • First sentence of each = topic sentence (one main idea only)
  • Then reason + example (after the topic sentence)

Closing

  • Sum up key message
  • End with a thought or call to action
  • No letter greeting or sign-off

Skeleton

Opening

[Catchy title]
[Hook]. Nowadays, [topic] has become [brief context].
[Thesis — main angle].

Body

First, [main idea 1]. This is important because [reason]. For instance, [example].
Second, [main idea 2]. Some people think [view A], while others believe [view B].
Third, [main idea 3 / tip]. If we [action], we can [benefit].

Closing

To conclude, [restate angle].
It is hoped that [final thought / call to action].

Useful phrases

  • Have you ever wondered why…?
  • In recent years, more and more people have…
  • The main reason is that…
  • Another important point is…
  • Critics argue that… On the other hand,…
  • A practical step students can take is…
  • All in all, it is clear that…
  • Perhaps it is time we…
