Proposal

Suggest a plan and persuade the reader to approve it.

P2 / P3B: Both (common in P3B). Check Data File for purpose, budget, dates, and who decides.

PAT
PurposeSuggest a plan and persuade the reader to approve it.
AudiencePrincipal, teacher-in-charge, committee, or organisation.
ToneFormal, practical, polite, confident — what / why / how / benefits.
Structure

Opening

  • Title: Proposal for [plan]
  • Who you are (if needed)
  • Why now (brief context — before the thesis)
  • End with thesis — the plan in one clear sentence (last sentence of the opening)

Body

  • Use short headings (2–4 sections)
  • First sentence under each = topic sentence (one main idea / aim only)
  • Then details, reasons and examples (after the topic sentence)
  • Typical flow: Aims → details → resources / schedule → benefits (and problems if asked)

Closing

  • Ask for approval
  • Offer to discuss further
  • Name / role if required
Skeleton

Slots: [thesis / angle] · [topic / point] · [detail / example] · [other]

Opening

Proposal for [name of plan]
[Why now — brief context].
I propose that [plan] be held [when / where] in order to [main aim].

Body

Aims
1. [aim 1]
2. [aim 2]

Details of the plan
- Target group: [who]
- Activities: [what]
- Date / time / venue: [details]
- Resources: [people / materials / budget]

Expected benefits
[Benefit 1]. [Benefit 2].

Closing

I hope this proposal will be approved.
I am happy to provide further details if needed.
[Name / role]
Useful Phrases
  • I am writing to propose that…
  • The aim of this proposal is to…
  • It is suggested that we…
  • The programme would take place on / from…
  • Participants would…
  • The estimated cost would be…
  • One expected benefit is that…
  • I should be grateful if you would consider this proposal.
